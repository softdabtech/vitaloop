# EXECUTION SPEC #5: COPY GUIDE - SAFE HEALTH CLAIMS

**Purpose:** Ensure all user-facing copy is legally safe + clinically honest  
**Scope:** Results page, protocols, emails, notifications  
**Review level:** Legal review before launch

---

## FUNDAMENTAL RULES

### Rule 1: Never Diagnose
```
❌ WRONG: "You have iron deficiency anemia"
✅ RIGHT: "Your ferritin is low (12, normal 30-150)"

❌ WRONG: "You have depression"
✅ RIGHT: "Low vitamin D may affect mood"
```

### Rule 2: Stick to Reference Ranges
```
❌ WRONG: "Optimal ferritin is 75"
✅ RIGHT: "Normal range is 30-150 (lab's reference)"

❌ WRONG: "You need more iron immediately"
✅ RIGHT: "Your ferritin is below normal range"
```

### Rule 3: Use Soft Language
```
❌ WRONG: "This causes your fatigue"
✅ RIGHT: "This may contribute to fatigue"

❌ WRONG: "Take this supplement"
✅ RIGHT: "Consider discussing supplementation with your doctor"
```

### Rule 4: Always Include Doctor Pathway
```
❌ WRONG: "Eat more spinach and you'll feel better"
✅ RIGHT: "Discuss iron sources and supplementation with your doctor"
```

### Rule 5: Educational Disclaimer
```
Every health claim must be followed by:
⚠️ "This is educational information. 
   Your doctor should diagnose and determine treatment."
```

### FERRITIN (Iron Storage)

**Safe explanation:**
```
"Ferritin measures iron stored in your body. Iron is needed for:
• Energy and reducing fatigue
• Immune system function
• Hair and nail growth

Your ferritin: 12 μg/L
Lab reference range: 30-150 μg/L
Status: Low

This may suggest:
• Inadequate dietary iron
• Absorption issues (celiac, IBS, etc)
• Blood loss
• Pregnancy

What to discuss with your doctor:
✓ Iron supplementation (typical: 25-50mg daily)
✓ Dietary sources (red meat, spinach, beans, fortified cereals)
✓ Underlying causes (absorption testing, blood loss evaluation)
✓ Timeline for improvement (typically 6-8 weeks)

⚠️ Do not self-diagnose or treat without medical guidance."
```

### TSH / THYROID (Metabolic Control)

**Safe explanation (if normal):**
```
"Your TSH is normal (2.1 mIU/L, range 0.4-4.0).

This suggests your thyroid is working well.

Continue monitoring:
• Retest in 1-2 years (routine screening)
• Sooner if experiencing fatigue, weight changes, or mood changes"
```

**Safe explanation (if abnormal):**
```
"Your TSH is elevated at 5.2 (normal: 0.4-4.0).

This may suggest:
• Underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism)
• Thyroid nodules
• Hashimoto's disease
• Dietary iodine insufficiency

⚠️ TSH abnormalities REQUIRE DOCTOR FOLLOW-UP.
This is not something to treat without professional diagnosis.

Your doctor should:
✓ Check free T3, free T4 levels
✓ Evaluate thyroid antibodies (if appropriate)
✓ Consider ultrasound (if indicated)
✓ Discuss treatment options (medication, lifestyle, monitoring)

IMPORTANT: Do NOT start iodine supplementation without testing.
Excess iodine can worsen thyroid issues."
```

**Safe explanation (normal):**

```
This suggests healthy blood sugar control.

Maintain this with:
• Regular physical activity
• Balanced diet (complex carbs, fiber, protein)
• Stable meal timing
• Adequate sleep
• Stress management"
```

**Safe explanation (prediabetic range):**
```
"Your fasting glucose is 112 mg/dL (normal: 70-100, prediabetic: 100-125).

This may indicate:
• Higher risk for type 2 diabetes
• Need for lifestyle changes
• Possible metabolic syndrome

Your doctor should:
✓ Confirm with additional tests (A1C, glucose tolerance test)
✓ Assess family history
✓ Evaluate other risk factors (weight, blood pressure, lipids)
✓ Recommend lifestyle changes (diet, exercise)

⚠️ Diabetes requires professional diagnosis and management.
This is not a diagnosis, only an observation."
```

## PROTOCOL / ACTION PLAN WORDING

### For Low Markers (Deficiencies)

**Format:**
```
[MARKER NAME] is low.

LIFESTYLE CHANGES (Priority: do first)
✓ [Specific action] - [Why this helps]
  Example: "Eat red meat 2-3x per week" - "Best source of heme iron, easily absorbed"
✓ [Specific action] - [Why this helps]

SUPPLEMENTATION (To discuss with doctor)
⚠️ [Supplement name]: [dose range] [frequency]
   • Why: [What it does]
   • When: [Best time to take]
   • Side effects: [Common effects, usually minor]
   • Interactions: [Foods or meds to avoid with this]

TIMELINE
• Duration: [How long to take it]
• Retest: [When to check again]
• Expected improvement: [Realistic timeline, e.g., 6-8 weeks]

DOCTOR DISCUSSION POINTS
• Ask about: [What to verify with doctor]
• Rule out: [Underlying conditions to check for]
• Monitor: [What to watch for]
```

### For High Markers (Excess)

**Format:**
```
[MARKER NAME] is elevated.

LIFESTYLE CHANGES (Most important)
✓ [Reduce intake / Increase activity] - [Why]
✓ [Behavioral change] - [Expected impact]

FOODS TO REDUCE
• [Specific foods high in this marker]
• [Processed items if relevant]
• [Beverages if relevant]

WHEN TO SEE DOCTOR
⚠️ This may indicate:
• [Possible cause #1]
• [Possible cause #2]
• [Less likely but possible cause]

Your doctor should check for:
• [Underlying condition #1]
• [Underlying condition #2]
• [Context-dependent factors]

TIMELINE
• Retest: [Timeframe for follow-up]
• Monitoring: [How often to check]

⚠️ Do not attempt to lower this with extreme measures.
Work with your doctor to find sustainable approach."
```

### For Normal Markers

**Format:**
```
[MARKER NAME] is normal (value, normal range).

This is good. Continue:
• [Current healthy habit]
• [Another habit to maintain]

Retest: [Timeline for routine re-check]"
```

## CLAIM AUDIT CHECKLIST

Before publishing ANY health claim:

- [ ] Does it diagnose? (If yes, remove it)
- [ ] Does it claim to cure? (If yes, change "will" to "may")
- [ ] Is it personalized to user's lab results? (If yes, general first)
- [ ] Does it suggest doctor involvement? (If no, add it)
- [ ] Would a doctor agree with this wording? (If unsure, simplify)
- [ ] Is there a disclaimer nearby? (If no, add ⚠️)
- [ ] Did we test this wording with 5 people? (If no, do it)
- [ ] Is this based on user's actual data? (If no, make it educational)
- [ ] Could this be misinterpreted as medical advice? (If yes, reword)
- [ ] Is the source credible? (If unsure, cite reference)

## REFERENCE LANGUAGE (Reuse These)

### Safety Disclaimers (Always Include)

```
⚠️ Educational Information:
This is educational information based on your lab results.
It is not medical advice. Your doctor should diagnose and determine treatment.

⚠️ Supplement Warning:
Supplements can interact with medications. Always discuss with your doctor
before starting any supplement, especially if taking prescriptions.

⚠️ Medical Supervision:
This is not a substitute for professional medical care.
If you experience worsening symptoms, contact your doctor immediately.

⚠️ Individual Variation:
Your body is unique. Response to supplementation varies by person.
Follow your doctor's guidance, not just general recommendations.
```

### Safe Action Language

```
"may help with"  — Instead of "cures" or "treats"
"consider discussing"  — Instead of "you should take"
"talk to your doctor about"  — Instead of "take this supplement"
"may affect"  — Instead of "causes"
"may be related to"  — Instead of "is caused by"
"some sources recommend"  — Instead of "you need"
"generally associated with"  — Instead of "directly caused by"
"appears to support"  — Instead of "improves"
"suggests"  — Instead of "confirms"
```

### Doctor Involvement Language

```
"Discuss with your doctor before starting"
"Your doctor should check for..."
"Talk to your doctor about whether..."
"Work with your healthcare provider to..."
"This may warrant doctor follow-up..."
"Your doctor can confirm..."
"Ask your doctor about..."
"Your healthcare team should evaluate..."
```

## EXAMPLES: BEFORE & AFTER

### Example 1: Ferritin

**BEFORE (Too aggressive):**
```
"You have iron deficiency anemia. 
Take our iron supplement now to cure your fatigue.
Your doctor will confirm the diagnosis."
```

**AFTER (Safe):**
```
"Your ferritin is 12 (lab's normal range: 30-150).

This means:
Low iron may contribute to fatigue, weak immunity, and hair loss.

Your doctor should:
✓ Confirm iron deficiency (not just low ferritin)
✓ Identify underlying cause
✓ Discuss supplementation or dietary changes
✓ Plan retest timeline

⚠️ This is educational. Your doctor diagnoses and treats."
```

---

### Example 2: Vitamin D

**BEFORE (Making claims):**
```
"Low vitamin D causes depression and weak bones.
Supplement with 4,000 IU daily for 30 days.
You'll feel better immediately."
```

**AFTER (Safe):**
```
"Your vitamin D is 18 ng/mL (normal: 30-100).

Low vitamin D may be related to:
• Mood effects (some research suggests a link)
• Bone health
• Immune function

What to discuss with your doctor:
✓ Adequate sun exposure (15-30 min daily)
✓ Vitamin D3 supplementation (typical: 1000-2000 IU daily)
✓ Dietary sources (fatty fish, fortified milk)
✓ Retest timing (8-12 weeks to assess response)

⚠️ Effects of supplementation vary by person.
Work with your doctor to determine right dose."
```

## SUMMARY

**Golden Rule:** If you're not 100% sure a claim is safe, ask a lawyer.

**Safe writing:**
- ✅ Educational (explain what the marker means)
- ✅ Data-driven (reference user's actual results)
- ✅ Doctor-involving (always suggest talking to doctor)
- ✅ Soft language (may, might, suggest, consider)
- ✅ Disclaimed (⚠️ included)

**Unsafe writing:**
- ❌ Diagnostic (claiming to diagnose)
- ❌ Prescriptive (telling user to take something)
- ❌ Claiming to cure (will, guaranteed, treat)
- ❌ Generic (not tied to user's actual data)
- ❌ Without disclaimers (no warnings)

This guide is the **legal safety net** for Vitaloop's health claims.
